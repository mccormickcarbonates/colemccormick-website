About Me

I am a carbonate sedimentologist and geochemist broadly interested in how fluids — seawater, burial brines, and hydrothermal fluids — move through and chemically transform sedimentary rocks, and in how the minerals those fluids leave behind can be read as archives of Earth's past environments. My work sits at the intersection of sedimentology, structural geology, and geochemistry, combining field observation, experimental petrology, and advanced isotope and trace-element techniques to answer questions that span from the chemistry of the ancient oceans to the physics of rock fracture.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar at The Pennsylvania State University, working with Dr. Kimberly V. Lau to experimentally constrain how carbonate geochemical proxies — particularly uranium and sulfur isotopes — record the redox evolution of ancient seawater, and how that signal can be altered during dolomitization and diagenesis. I completed my Ph.D. in Earth and Environmental Sciences at The University of Manchester (2023), advised by Dr. Cathy Hollis and Dr. Ernest H. Rutter, where I studied fault-controlled hydrothermal dolomite bodies in the Rocky Mountains of western Canada using an integrated toolkit of hyperspectral imaging, basin-scale structural analysis, U–Pb geochronology, and rock mechanics. Before that, I earned my M.Sc. (2019) and B.Sc. Honours in Geology (2017) at the University of Alberta, where my research on the Cayman Islands first drew me into carbonate diagenesis.

My research has been recognized with the Ramsey Medal (Earth and Planetary Science Letters) for the year's most outstanding publication in structural geology & tectonics, the Harold Reading Medal (Basin Research) for the most outstanding publication by a graduate student, and the BP Prize for best presentation at the Tectonic Studies Group Annual General Meeting. Alongside my own research, I mentor graduate and undergraduate students on experimental geochemistry and carbonate petrography, and I am actively seeking a tenure-track faculty position where I can continue to build a research program bridging sedimentary geochemistry and structural geology, and teach the next generation of Earth scientists.
